## Problem Statement
A needle-in-a-haystack meme went viral on PC in August 2026: a clip of a 5-million-piece haystack with one needle passed 48M views. Both PC games built on it (Needle In A Haystack Simulator by Studio BitDot, and Find The Needle, a dig-then-automate game) are PC only and launch in Q4 2026. On Google Play the only options are fast clones rated about 1.1–2.5★ with about 10K installs. Haystack Co. is the first good mobile version. You dig a generated 2D haystack by hand, sell the hay, and buy tools and then machines until automation finds the needles. It's a relaxing, ad-supported idle game for adults (Play target audience 18+, as with Gemmory), built solo with a 3–4 week MVP.

## Demand Evidence
- About 300 strangers asked the founder personally for a phone version. They can't be contacted again, so there is no launch list.
- Trend: the viral clip (48M+ views), 9+ Steam clones, and Roblox clones. One report says a Roblox clone made about $100K/month; that figure is unverified.
- Missing: no evidence about behavior (installs, retention, pre-registrations). The rake-spike clip is the first test that measures behavior.

## Status Quo
They watch the clips and wait for the PC games. The real competitor is a 15-second video, so the first 60 seconds of the game has to feel better than watching: *you* are digging, and the needle-found moment is good enough to clip.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **Player: the wind-down grinder.** An adult playing in the evening or in bed, 5–15 minute sessions, sound on, phone held upright. Likes watching numbers and machines grow. Relaxation is the product and automation is the reward.
- **Wedge:** one pile you rake by hand, a needle within about 60 seconds, a "clink" with glint and haptic, sell the hay, five tools (rake, pitchfork, magnet, leaf blower, metal detector; CEO D2), and one conveyor that digs on its own by about minute 8–10. Everything else is built on top of that loop.

## Constraints
- Android and Google Play first, solo developer, MVP in 3–4 weeks.
- Play target audience set to 18+ (not an IARC rating), to stay out of the Families policy. See Game Rules for the appeals-to-children mitigation.
- Must not feel like a Gemmory sibling. Reusing code is fine; reusing the concept is not.
- Must have a sense of progress (by hand → upgrades → automation) and be hands-on in every session.
- Never require an ad to progress.
- Play account is personal. The founder says the 14-day, 12-tester closed test was already passed for Gemmory. Whether that carries over to a new app is unverified (see Open Questions).

## Premises
1. The meme brings the install and idle progression keeps the player. The first pile gives a needle in about 60 s, and the first machine arrives in the first session (about 10 min). *Kept after the second opinion challenged it (see below).*
2. The player is the wind-down grinder: portrait, sound on, 5–15 min. The ASMR hay sound and the needle "clink" are MVP scope.
3. Ads never gate progress. An interstitial shows only after a needle is found, with a cap. Rewarded ads are always optional.
4. 2D top-down in KMP + Compose reusing Gemmory, not a game engine. This depends on the rake spike passing on the Galaxy A06. If it fails, switch to Godot before building anything else.
5. Distribution starts from zero: Play pre-registration goes up early (or a fallback link, see Open Question 2), clips get posted from day 1, and the launch lands alongside the PC Q4 releases (window under Distribution Plan).
6. Gemmory's code is copied into BrewingBytes/HaystackCo, not shared as a library.

## Approaches Considered
- **A: Lean Compose MVP.** One app module with a hard-coded economy. Rejected: idle-game balance can't be tuned without an app update.
- **C: Godot 4 with physics hay.** Rejected: loses nearly all Gemmory reuse and puts a new engine plus plugins into a 4-week window.

## Recommended Approach
**B: a pure simulation core with a remote-tunable economy (chosen).**
- `shared` (KMP, pure `commonMain`, enforced by Gemmory's purity check), all unit-tested:
  - **Pile:** a deterministic pile simulation. A density grid is generated from a seed, needles are placed from the seed, and the rake brush subtracts and pushes density aside.
  - **Economy:** tools and machines as data (cost curves, dig rate, magnet radius), through a tool/machine **type registry** so new tools and automation machines can be added later as catalog data plus one behavior class each, without changing the sim core (CEO D2).
  - **Offline earnings:** computed from a wall-clock delta, capped.
  - **Saves:** a save codec in Gemmory's pattern; the grid is run-length encoded in commonMain (Gemmory has no deflate, and commonMain has no zip API).
- **Pacing targets as tests:** with default play, a simulated player finds the first needle within ≤60 s and can afford the first machine within ≤10 min.
- **Balance tables** ship as a bundled JSON economy, with **Firebase Remote Config overrides** for tuning without an app update (CEO D12, replacing the custom signed catalog).
- **`androidApp`** (Compose):
  - **Rendering:** an AGSL `RuntimeShader` draws the hay; **minSdk 33**, no Canvas fallback (CEO D13). The look is **2D top-down with a pseudo-3D shade**: the shader turns density into height, lighting and shadow so the pile reads as 3D, with a zoom/pan camera and no orbit (CEO D6).
  - **Feel:** strand particles near the finger, a rustle tied to brush speed (new runtime audio work: Gemmory only plays pre-rendered SoundPool clips from `tools/make_sounds.py`), and a needle "clink" with glint and haptic.
  - **Copied from Gemmory:** AdMob + UMP + ad policy (rewarded 2× sell, 2× offline earnings and detector hint; interstitial only after a needle, capped), Play Billing (no-ads), Firebase Analytics + Crashlytics with UMP-gated consent (CEO D7), the sound synth, and the store-frame pipeline.
- **Deferred:** in-game share replay (CEO D3; marketing clips are screen-recorded), Golden Needle prestige, daily seeded pile with a leaderboard, Harvest Pass, co-op, localization beyond English (the pipeline is ready to copy).

## Game Rules (MVP defaults; /plan-eng-review confirms the numbers)
- **Pile lifecycle.** Pile *n* is generated from `seed(n)`. All counts and sizes come from the catalog: by catalog default pile 1 holds 1 needle, placed near the center of the pile (where real players rake first), not tuned to the simulated path, and the count grows with *n*. A pile is complete when all its needles are found. The next, bigger pile then unlocks, and leftover hay in the finished pile can't be harvested. Only the current pile exists in a save.
- **Grid persistence.** The save holds the seed plus the current 512×1024 density grid, run-length encoded (raw 512 KB; cleared areas are long runs of zero). It's written on pause, every 30 s, and when a pile completes, atomically (Gemmory's `AtomicFiles`), keeping the previous good save as a backup. On a decode failure the game loads the backup, quarantines the bad file, logs a Crashlytics non-fatal and shows "Restored your last save"; if both fail it starts fresh and says so (CEO D11). A save-codec test bounds the size (e.g. ≤ 150 KB for a half-cleared pile) and checks the round trip.
- **Needle found** is one event, `NeedleFound`, and it is the only trigger for the clink, glint, haptic and interstitial eligibility:
  - Rake, pitchfork, blower and machines: a needle is found when the mean density over its reveal cell (a square of `revealCellSize` grid units around the needle, catalog value, default 8) drops below the reveal threshold (catalog value).
  - Magnet: the needle is found if its center lies within the magnet radius during a drag, regardless of density.
  - Metal detector (a tool the player buys and holds; CEO D2): never finds a needle. It only beeps louder as you get closer. The rewarded "detector hint" is a one-shot ping from the same logic, free of the tool purchase.
- **NeedleFound sources.** The event carries `source = Player | Machine | Offline`.
  - The clink, glint and haptic play for every source.
  - Interstitial eligibility applies to `Player` and `Machine` finds only.
- **Machines and offline.**
  - Machines belong to the player, not to a pile, and carry over to every new pile. They remove density along their own dig pattern, so they produce hay and can uncover needles.
  - Offline, the sim fast-forwards the machines' dig over the elapsed time and **may cross pile boundaries**. When the current pile completes offline, pile *n+1* is generated and the machines keep digging it with the remaining time. At most `offlinePileCompletions` piles (catalog, default 1) complete per offline credit, after which accrual stops.
  - Needles uncovered while you were away are credited on return in a "while you were away" reveal (`source = Offline`), which plays the clink.
  - A unit test covers an offline credit that completes a pile and continues into the next one, and one that hits the completion limit.
  - **Resume budget (CEO D8):** the offline fast-forward runs in coarse analytic steps (default 1-minute chunks), off the main thread, deterministic for the same save and delta, and finishes in < 500 ms on the Galaxy A06. A Test Lab benchmark covers the 8 h, pile-crossing case.
- **Offline time.** `delta = clamp(now − lastSeen, 0, cap)`, with the cap a catalog value (default 8 h).
  - A clock moved backwards (negative delta) gives 0 and resets `lastSeen` to now.
  - A clock moved forwards is bounded by the cap.
  - The rewarded "2× offline" doubles hay only, never needles.
  - Unit tests cover a negative delta, a zero delta, a delta at the cap, and a delta past the cap.
  - **Clock guard (CEO D5):** total credited offline time per calendar day is capped (`dailyOfflineCap`, catalog, default 12 h); within a session, time comes from `elapsedRealtime` (monotonic); the ad ledger's day only moves forward, so setting the clock back can't reset the 6-per-day interstitial cap. Unit tests cover forward-then-back repeats and a ledger rollback attempt.
- **Interstitial policy.** Hard-coded ceilings that the catalog can only tighten:
  - none in the first session;
  - none before the first machine is bought;
  - at least 4 min between interstitials;
  - at most 1 per completed pile and 6 per day;
  - never after an offline find or the scripted first-magnet tutorial find (the first time a player uses the magnet, a needle is placed in reach).
  - Covered by the ad policy tests, copying Gemmory's daily-ledger pattern.
- **Catalog versioning and saves.**
  - Each catalog carries `economyVersion` and `minAppVersion`.
  - Item ids are append-only and never removed. The save stores owned item ids and counts, never prices or rates.
  - On load, the current catalog's rates apply to owned items. Owned items are always kept, and prices only affect future purchases.
  - **Source (CEO D12):** the economy is a bundled JSON file; Firebase Remote Config may override it. Remote values are fetched in the background and activated only at the next cold start (CEO D10); the save records the active `economyVersion`, so a session and an offline credit always use one economy.
  - Precedence: a Remote Config economy is used only if it parses and validates against the schema, its `minAppVersion` ≤ the running app's version (also targeted with RC app-version conditions), and its `economyVersion` ≥ the bundled copy's. Otherwise the last activated valid economy or the bundled copy is used. This applies offline too.
  - A save-codec test loads a save against a catalog with changed rates and with a new item added. Selection tests check that an economy with too high a `minAppVersion`, or one that fails schema validation, is ignored.
- **Pacing guarantee (CEO D12).** The pacing simulation runs in CI against the bundled economy and as a required pre-publish check (`./gradlew :shared:checkPacing -Peconomy=<file>`) before any Remote Config change: first needle within ≤ 60 s, first machine affordable within ≤ 10 min. This is now a manual gate, not enforced by a signer.
  - **Simulated default player:** 4 brush strokes per second along a raster path over the pile with the rake, sells every time hay ≥ the price of the cheapest upgrade not yet owned, and buys that upgrade straight away (greedy).
- **Share clip (deferred, CEO D3).** No in-game share in the MVP; marketing clips are screen-recorded. The deterministic sim keeps a later replay share possible (see TODOS.md).
- **Play target audience and ads.**
  - Target audience in the Play Console is **18+**, not an IARC rating. AdMob/UMP are configured as not child-directed, as in Gemmory.
  - Risk: Play's "unintentionally appeals to children" review.
  - Mitigation: grown-up store art (photographic or illustrated hay, no cartoon mascots or kid characters), a dry adult tone in the copy, and no kid-styled UI.
- **Secrets in a public repo.**
  - Haystack Co. gets its own upload keystore. There is no catalog signing key (CEO D12).
  - Private keys and keystores live only in `local.properties` (gitignored) and in GitHub Actions secrets.
  - Before Gemmory code is copied in, the first commit adds a `.gitignore` covering keystores, `local.properties`, `google-services.json` and `*.pem`, plus a CI secret scan (gitleaks) that fails the build.
  - AdMob app and unit IDs aren't secret (Gemmory commits them), and they stay in `gradle.properties` with Gemmory's release-ID validation.
  - The repo stays public at the founder's choice.

### R2-1 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The share replay cannot work as written. To re-simulate the last ~8 s you need the grid state from about 8 s before the find, plus every brush input since then. The save is written every 30 s, on pause and when a pile completes (that is, after the find), so 'the saved state' is either up to 30 s old or already contains the find. The replay also requires brush inputs to be logged continuously during play, which contradicts 'Nothing is recorded during play'.

**Remedy**

> Specify the replay source: a rolling input ring buffer that is always on, plus either a grid snapshot kept at least about 8 s back, or a re-simulation from the last 30 s save (up to about 38 s of sim) together with the inputs and machine ticks since that save. State the cost in memory and CPU, and reword 'nothing is recorded' to say that inputs are logged but no frames are captured.

### R2-2 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> NeedleFound triggers the share offer, and offline machine finds are credited on return with a reveal and a clink. Nothing says whether an offline find (or a find by a machine during play) raises NeedleFound, and therefore the share offer. An offline find has no last ~8 s of brush inputs to replay, so a share button there has nothing to show.

**Remedy**

> State whether offline and machine finds emit NeedleFound, or a separate event, and whether they offer Share. If they do, define what the clip shows (for example, a replay of the machine's final dig before the find).

### Section 1: Architecture Review
```
                       +---------------------------- androidApp (Compose) ----------------------------+
                       |  PlayScreen ── AgslHayRenderer (minSdk 33, D13)                              |
                       |      │            ▲ dirty rows                                               |
   touch/drag ────────▶|  InputMapper ──▶ │                     ShopScreen   AwayReveal   Settings    |
                       |      │       GameController (main loop, 60 Hz tick, owns Session)            |
                       |      │            │   │        │              │                              |
                       |  SoundBank   Haptics  AdsGate(AdMob+UMP)  BillingGate  Analytics(Firebase)    |
                       |                   │                                                          |
                       |  SaveStore(AtomicFiles, backup)  EconomyStore(bundled + Remote Config, D12)   |
                       +-------------------│---------------------------------│----------------------+
                                           ▼                                 ▼
  +------------------------------------ shared / commonMain (pure, no android.*) -----------------------+
  |  PileSim(grid, seed, needles) ◀── ToolRegistry {Rake,Pitchfork,Magnet,Blower,Detector}              |
  |  MachineRegistry {Conveyor, …}   Economy(catalog tables)   OfflineSim(coarse steps, daily cap)       |
  |  AdPolicy + DailyLedger (forward-only day)   SaveCodec(seed, RLE grid, owned ids, ledgers)            |
  |  EconomyCodec + schema validation + selection(minAppVersion, economyVersion)                         |
  +------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  jvmMain tools: checkPacing (PacingSimulator over an economy file; CI + pre-publish), seed/needle preview.
```
- **Boundaries:** the sim never touches Android. Renderers read the grid, they never write it. **OK**
- **Coupling (new):** ToolRegistry/MachineRegistry are the extension seam D2 asked for. Each type is catalog data plus one `ToolBehavior`/`MachineBehavior` class. **OK**
- **Pile state machine:**
```
   Generating ──▶ Active ──(all needles found)──▶ Complete ──▶ Generating(n+1)
                    │  ▲                                   (offline may run Active→Complete→Generating once,
                    ▼  │                                    capped by offlinePileCompletions)
                  Paused (app background; save written)
   Invalid: Complete→Active of the same pile (leftover hay can't be harvested); prevented: PileSim drops grid on Complete.
```
- **Economy state machine (D10, D12):** `Bundled ──RC fetch+validate──▶ Staged ──cold start──▶ Active`. A validation failure keeps the current one. Staged never activates mid-session.
- **Scaling:** single-player client; Remote Config is served by Firebase. **OK**
- **Single points of failure:** Firebase Remote Config availability (mitigated: the bundled economy).
- **Rollback:** a bad economy is fixed by rolling back the Remote Config version in the Firebase console (takes effect next cold start). A bad app goes through a Play staged rollout halt plus a hotfix. `minAppVersion` and RC conditions keep new data off old clients.

### Section 4: Data Flow & Interaction Edge Cases
```
  TOUCH ─▶ InputMapper ─▶ Tool.apply(grid) ─▶ needle check ─▶ Economy.addHay ─▶ (dirty rows) ─▶ Renderer
    │ shadow: two fingers = pan/zoom, never dig; touch outside pile = no-op; 120 Hz input = coalesced per tick
  REMOTE ─▶ RC fetch ─▶ parse + schema ─▶ select(minAppVersion, economyVersion) ─▶ Staged ─▶ next cold start
    │ shadow: empty/throttled → keep active; wrong version → ignored; invalid → keep + log
  RESUME ─▶ OfflineSim(delta, dailyCap) ─▶ AwayReveal ─▶ Save
    │ shadow: delta<0 → 0; >cap → cap; daily cap reached → 0; pile crossed → once, then stop
```
**Async ordering (shared state: Session + save file).** Invariant: every save holds one consistent snapshot (grid, hay and owned items from the same tick).
```
  tick N (main)          SaveStore (IO)                 OfflineSim (bg)
  copy snapshot S(N) ─▶  encode S(N) … write tmp→rename
  tick N+1 mutates grid  (unaffected: works on the copy)
                                                         only runs before Session starts (resume gate)
```
- The save always encodes a **copy** taken on the main thread, so tick N+1 can't tear it. OfflineSim runs before the Session takes input (a resume gate), so the two never overlap.
- Regression test: pause the encoder mid-write while ticks mutate, then decode and assert it equals S(N).

| Interaction | Edge case | Handled? | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy upgrade | double tap | Y | button debounced; purchase is idempotent per tick |
| Rewarded "2× sell" | app backgrounded mid-ad | Y | reward only on callback; the timer runs on monotonic time |
| Rewarded hint | no needle left in pile | Y | button hidden when the pile has 0 unfound needles |
| Interstitial | shown while finger is down | Y | queued until the gesture ends, then subject to policy |
| Pile complete | app killed during transition | Y | save on Complete before generating n+1 |
| Offline reveal | 0 hay earned | Y | reveal skipped; no empty dialog |

### Section 4: Performance (eng)
- **Memory:** grid 512 KB + A8 bitmap 512 KB + 2 reused snapshot buffers (512 KB each, double-buffered, no allocation per save) ≈ 2 MB steady [estimated]. Particles about 2K × 16 B. No per-frame allocation in `Session.tick`; strokes go in a reused ring.
- **Texture upload:** the grid is copied into the A8 bitmap once per frame, only when a dirty rect exists. It's bound with `RuntimeShader.setInputBuffer` (API 33), which skips color management for data textures (Android RuntimeShader reference). Full 512 KB upload assumed (D13); the spike measures it.

- **Save:** RLE encode of 512 KB on the IO dispatcher every 30 s; fsync via AtomicFiles. No network on the hot path; RC fetch runs once per launch in the background.
- **Caching:** the brush falloff lookup table is computed once per radius from integers (determinism rule).
